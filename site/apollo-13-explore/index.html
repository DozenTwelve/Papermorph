    How the drawing is made

    The drawing looks down on the Moon’s orbit, with Earth held still in the middle. The grey track is where the Moon really was, hour by hour, from NASA JPL’s Horizons system; its dots mark each midnight (UTC). The distance under the clock is from Earth’s centre to the Moon’s centre.

    Apollo 13’s line is a sketch, not its real track. At the moments NASA listed in its Mission Report (Table 4-II), the line is as far from Earth as NASA measured; which way it points in between is drawn by hand. Near the Moon it is drawn wide, so the loop around the far side can be seen, and near Earth it is lifted clear of the enlarged Earth. Earth is drawn about 3 times bigger than it is, the Moon about 7½ times. The three paths are compared as ideas: nothing here predicts what a different burn would have done.

    MET is mission elapsed time, the mission clock: hours, minutes and seconds since launch, 11 April 1970 at 19:13:00 UTC.
