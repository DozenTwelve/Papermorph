# Symbols and diagrams that change

For abstract and procedural ideas (the maths books on the showcase site). The picture explains through change: things are rearranged, split, balanced, counted and compared while the narration says why. The change is the argument, so each move must be one the idea allows.

- **One reading line.** A derivation or a procedure reads in one direction, each step under or beside the last, aligned on what stays the same (the `=` sign, the place values). The eye never searches for the next step.
- **Change the thing, don't replace it.** Tokens that combine move together and collapse into the result (`tokens`, `collapse`); a tile pair cancels where it stands; a dot hops along the number line. A fresh line of text is a weaker picture than the old one transforming.
- **Exact where it matters.** Number lines, grids, areas and graphs are to scale; illustrative pictures only need to be clear. Approximations are written as approximations (≈ 4.47…, never = 4.47).
- **Show the common mistake** where one exists (squaring the coefficient too), visibly crossed out, then the right way.
- **Vary the composition with the content.** A chapter that is one centred formula after another feels monotonous; a number line, a table, a picture of the quantity and a worked line each have their own layout.

Storyboard these beats like any other: place the key moments in the real page and capture them with `strip.py` before timing the moves ([scenes.md](scenes.md#storyboard-layout-first-then-motion)). The engine's maths helpers (number lines, tiles, fraction rows, planes, data plots) are in `lib/math.js`, listed in [engine.md](engine.md#drawing).
